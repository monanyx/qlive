Documentation

QuantumLive is a static, build-free web app that streams public market data from crypto exchanges straight into your browser, renders it as a trading terminal, and lets you paper-trade against it. This page explains what every part does and exactly how the numbers are produced.

Getting started

Open the terminal, pick a market and a venue, and you're streaming. Nothing to install, no account.

Run it locally

The site uses native JavaScript modules, which browsers refuse to load from file:// URLs, so serve the folder over HTTP:

git clone https://github.com/monanyx/qlive.git
cd qlive
npm start            # zero-dependency dev server on http://localhost:8080
# or: python3 -m http.server 8080

Deploy

Any static host works. On GitHub Pages: Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Deploy from a branch, choose the branch and the / (root) folder. All paths are relative, so the site works under a project sub-path such as /qlive/.

Deep links

The terminal keeps its state in the URL, so links are shareable:

terminal.html?symbol=ETH&exchange=kraken&tf=300

symbol ∈ BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE · exchange ∈ coinbase, binance, kraken, sim · tf ∈ 1, 60, 300, 900, 3600 (seconds).

Terminal tour

Header & stats bar

Choose the market and the data source. The connection pill shows the feed state — Connecting, Subscribing, Live, Reconnecting — and turns purple when you are on the simulator. If a live venue falls back to the simulator, a Retry live button appears. The stats bar shows last price, 24h change/high/low/volume, mid, spread (absolute and in basis points), the share of taker volume that bought during the last minute, and the median feed lag.

Order book

  • Grouping merges levels into price buckets (bids round down, asks round up, so no bucket straddles the spread).
  • Depth bars show cumulative size from the touch on a scale shared by both sides.
  • Imbalance at the bottom compares resting size in the top 10 levels: (bid − ask) / (bid + ask).
  • Click a row to copy its price into the ticket; Shift+click also copies the cumulative size. Amber dots mark your resting orders.

Chart views

  • Candles — 1s/1m/5m/15m/1h OHLCV built from live trades and back-filled from each venue's REST candles. Your fills appear as arrows, open orders as dashed lines, alerts as dotted lines. Alt+click sets an alert.
  • Depth — cumulative bid/ask curves around mid. Scroll or use ± to zoom between ±0.05 % and ±8 %; hover for cumulative size and notional to any price.
  • Heatmap — time runs left→right, price bottom→top, colour = resting size (log-scaled against an adaptive reference). Green/red lines are best bid/ask; bubbles are trades sized by notional. Watch liquidity get pulled, refilled and swept.

Activity & account

Trades is the time-and-sales tape (colour = aggressor side). Whales lists prints above the threshold set in Settings (default $50K). Liquidations streams forced orders from Binance USDⓈ-M futures (or the simulator's own). The account panel holds positions, open orders, history, fills, alerts and Diagnostics.

Data sources

Every connection is opened directly by your browser to the venue's public, unauthenticated endpoint. No API keys, no proxy.

VenueWebSocketChannelsBook model

Binance wss://stream.binance.com:9443/stream

Kraken wss://ws.kraken.com/v2 book (depth 25), trade, ticker Snapshot + updates; truncated to the subscribed depth after every update
Liquidations wss://fstream.binance.com/ws/<sym>@forceOrder forceOrder At most one (the latest) liquidation per symbol per second, by Binance's design

Candle history comes from the public REST endpoints: Coinbase /products/<id>/candles (no 1s granularity), Binance /api/v3/klines (incl. 1s) and Kraken /0/public/OHLC. If a request fails (network, CORS, region), the chart simply builds from live trades.

Conventions

  • Trade side is always the taker side. Coinbase reports the maker side in match messages, so it is inverted; Binance's m flag means "buyer is maker".
  • Binance quotes in USDT; it is labelled as such and treated as USD for paper trading.
  • All timestamps are shown in your local time zone.

Resilience

  • Reconnects use exponential backoff with jitter (1 s → 30 s) and cycle through alternate endpoints.
  • A watchdog reconnects if a stream goes quiet for 15 s; a crossed book that persists for 3 s triggers a resubscribe for a fresh snapshot.
  • If a venue delivers no market data within 9 s (blocked network, geo-restriction, outage) the terminal falls back to the simulator and tells you. This can be turned off in Settings.

Paper trading

Paper trading is simulated entirely in your browser. No order ever reaches an exchange, and no real money is involved.

Order types

TypeBehaviour
MarketWalks the visible opposite side level by level and fills at the volume-weighted average price. Rejected if visible depth is insufficient. Taker fee.
LimitIf it crosses the spread, it takes liquidity up to its limit price (taker) and the remainder rests. Options: Post-only (reject instead of taking) and IOC (never rest).
StopRests until the last trade price reaches the stop (buy stops above, sell stops below the market), then executes as a market order.

When resting limits fill

  • Full fill when the opposite best price reaches the limit (e.g. best ask ≤ buy limit).
  • Partial fill when a trade prints through the limit (strictly below a buy / above a sell), capped by the printed size.
  • Prints exactly at the limit do not fill: queue position is unknown, so the engine is deliberately conservative.
  • Resting fills are maker fills, executed at the limit price.

Accounting

  • A single USD cash balance (default $100,000, changeable on reset). Open orders reserve funds: buys reserve qty × price × (1 + taker fee) (stops add a 2 % buffer), sells reserve the asset.
  • Positions use an average-cost basis that includes fees. Selling realises proceeds − fee − avg cost × qty.
  • Equity marks every position to its latest mid price. Default fees are 0.10 % taker / 0.05 % maker (configurable).
  • The account is saved to localStorage and survives reloads. Orders only match while their market is open in the terminal.

Simulator model

The simulator (assets/js/sim/market-sim.js) is a stylised but internally consistent limit-order-book model. It advances in small time steps (25–100 ms), is deterministic for a given seed, and emits exactly the same normalised events as the live adapters — so every view, the paper engine and the latency monitor work identically on it.

1 · Fair price with stochastic volatility

A latent fair price follows geometric Brownian motion whose volatility is itself a log-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, with rare Poisson jumps that also kick volatility up (volatility clustering):

dlnP = σₜ dW − ½σₜ² dt + J dN, σₜ = σ̄ · e^hₜ, dh = −κ_h h dt + ν dB

2 · Spread dynamics

The target spread (in ticks) is an OU process whose mean widens with volatility. Makers continuously quote the target touch — except after a sweep empties it, when re-quoting waits an exponential time with mean refillTau. The visible spread therefore widens transiently after large market orders and mean-reverts.

ds = κ_s (μ_s (σₜ/σ̄)^γ − s) dt + η dW, s ≥ 1 tick

3 · Power-law depth

Resting orders arrive at a distance d from the touch drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution (dense near mid), with size decaying as a power law of distance plus lognormal noise. Occasionally an order is a "wall" (4–14× size) snapped to a round price. Levels are randomly resized or cancelled; stale quotes that the fair price moves through are pulled or, sometimes, traded through.

q(d) = Q_touch · (1 + d/x₀)^−α · LogNormal(−σ²/2, σ)

4 · Order flow

Taker orders arrive as a self-exciting Hawkes process (bursts of activity), with Pareto-distributed notional (rare whale prints) and a side tilted by short-term momentum. Each order walks the book, printing one trade per price level like an aggregated trade feed, and leaves square-root-law permanent impact.

λ(t) = μ + Σᵢ α e^(−β (t − tᵢ)), notional ~ Pareto(x_m, a), ΔlnP = ± k √(Q / Q_touch) bps

5 · Liquidations

A sharp move over 15 s (beyond 2.2σ) raises the rate of forced orders on the losing side. They hit the book as market orders, so cascades emerge naturally.

Per-symbol starting price, long-run volatility and touch notional live in assets/js/core/config.js. When you have seen a live price for a symbol, the simulator starts from it instead.

The unit tests check the model's invariants: the book never crosses, deltas reproduce the internal book exactly, prices sit on the tick grid, depth density decays away from mid, trade counts are over-dispersed (clustered) and heavy-tailed, and the spread mean-reverts after a sweep.

Latency monitor

Open Diagnostics in the terminal's account panel. Every stage keeps its last 1,024 samples in a ring buffer; percentiles are computed on demand.

StageWhat is measured
Feed lagExchange event timestamp → time the message was received. Includes network transit and any clock offset between your machine and the exchange.
ParseJSON.parse plus normalisation of one message (not applicable to the simulator).
ApplyApplying one normalised event to the book, tape or candles.
RenderAll work done in one animation frame to repaint the views that changed.
Frame intervalTime between consecutive painted frames (≈16.7 ms at 60 Hz).
Browsers deliberately coarsen performance.now() — to about 100 µs on ordinary pages, ~5 µs on cross-origin-isolated ones — to mitigate timing attacks. Sub-microsecond (let alone nanosecond) figures are not meaningful in a browser, so values are reported in µs/ms and a reading of "0 µs" means "below the timer's resolution".

On the simulator, feed lag comes from an emulated network hop (lognormal, median ≈ 30 ms, order-preserving), which you can disable in Settings. Rendering is decoupled from message rate: views mark themselves dirty and are repainted at most once per frame, so a burst of hundreds of messages costs one paint.

Architecture

Plain HTML, CSS and native ES modules — no framework and no build step. The only third-party code is TradingView Lightweight Charts™ (Apache-2.0), vendored locally along with the Inter and JetBrains Mono fonts (OFL).

index.html, terminal.html, markets.html, docs.html, 404.html
sw.js, manifest.webmanifest       offline support / installable PWA
assets/js/
  core/      config, formatting, storage, seeded RNG, event emitter
  feeds/     WebSocket base, exchange adapters, pure normalisers,
             REST history, feed manager (fallback), multi-ticker
  market/    order book (sorted levels), candles, market model
  sim/       market microstructure simulator
  trading/   paper-trading engine, price alerts
  metrics/   latency monitor (ring buffers, percentiles, rates)
  ui/        views: book, candles, depth, heatmap, tape, ticket,
             account, diagnostics; scheduler, theme, toasts
  pages/     one entry module per page
tests/       node:test unit tests + Playwright smoke test

The data path is venue → adapter (normalise) → market model → frame scheduler → views, with the paper engine and alerts subscribed to the market model. See the diagram on the home page.

Privacy & security

  • There is no backend. The only network traffic is to the exchanges' public endpoints listed above — no analytics, no trackers, no third-party fonts or CDNs.
  • Every page ships a strict Content-Security-Policy: scripts, styles and fonts only from the site itself, and network connections only to the listed exchange hosts.
  • Exchange data is written to the page with textContent, never parsed as HTML.
  • Settings, the paper account, alerts and your watchlist are stored in localStorage under keys prefixed qlive.; clear site data to remove them.

FAQ

Why does a venue fall back to the simulator?

The venue's WebSocket could not deliver data within 9 seconds. Common causes: Binance blocks some regions (including the US), corporate networks block WebSockets, or the venue is down. Try another venue or press Retry live.

Why is the liquidations tab empty?

Liquidations come from Binance futures, which may be blocked where you are, and they are bursty — quiet markets can go minutes without one. On the simulator they appear after sharp moves.

Opening index.html from disk shows a warning.

Browsers block JavaScript modules on file://. Run npm start (or any static server) instead.

Can I trade real money?

No. QuantumLive never connects to an account and has no way to place real orders.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once visited: a service worker caches the site, and the simulator needs no network. Install it from your browser's menu to use it like an app.

Market data belongs to the respective exchanges and may be delayed, incomplete or inaccurate. Nothing here is financial advice.
