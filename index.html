What's inside

A professional trading terminal, without the account.

Everything runs client-side: exchange WebSockets go straight from your browser to the venue, and all state lives in local storage. Here's what you get.

Live Level-2 order books

Full-depth books from Coinbase, Binance and Kraken with price grouping, cumulative depth bars, spread in bps and top-of-book imbalance.

How it works

One pipeline, any venue.

Each venue adapter normalises its wire format into the same events. A single market model applies them, and a frame scheduler repaints only what changed — at most once per display refresh, however fast messages arrive. Latency probes (amber) time every stage.

The simulator

A limit order book that behaves like one.

When a venue is unreachable — or you just want a sandbox — the terminal switches to an internally consistent market model. It is seeded with the last live price you saw and emits the exact same events as the exchanges.

1

Power-law depth

Resting size decays with distance from the touch, so liquidity concentrates near mid. Walls cluster on round numbers.

q(d) ∝ (1 + d/x₀)^−α · LogNormal
2

Spread dynamics

An Ornstein–Uhlenbeck spread whose mean widens with volatility; sweeps open transient gaps that makers refill.

ds = κ(μ(σ) − s)dt + η dW
3

Clustered order flow

Taker orders arrive as a self-exciting Hawkes process with Pareto-sized notional, tilted by short-term momentum.

λ(t) = μ + Σ α·e^(−β(t−tᵢ))
4

Impact & liquidations

Market orders walk the book and leave square-root-law impact; sharp moves trigger forced orders that cascade.

ΔP/P ≈ k·√(Q / Q_touch)
